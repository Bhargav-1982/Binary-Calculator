How to use

All values are 32 bits, the same size as a MIPS register. HEX, DEC, OCT, and BIN always show the same value.

Choose the input base

Tap HEX, DEC, OCT, or BIN, or tap a row in the display. The selected base key turns yellow. Only the digits of that base can be typed. The small word before the result (HEX, DEC, OCT, or BIN) shows the base of the number.

To type a negative decimal number, type the number and press NEG. DEC signed accepts up to 2147483647. For larger decimal numbers, tap the DEC unsigned row (up to 4294967295).

To get −2147483648 (the most negative 32-bit value), type 80000000 in HEX, or tap the DEC unsigned row and type 2147483648.

Two-value operations

Type the first value, press the operation, type the second value, then press =. If you press = without a second value, the first value is used again (5 + = gives 10).

AND OR XORBitwise AND, OR, XOR
NAND NOR XNORNOT of AND, OR, XOR
+ −Add and subtract. The result wraps to 32 bits. "Signed overflow" shows when the signed result of the last operation does not fit in 32 bits.

Binary column view

In BIN, after + or − and =, the display shows the work in columns: the first number, the second number, and the result, with bit numbers (31, 27, ..., 3, 0) on top. For +, the "carry" row on top shows the carry into each bit, and the line under the result shows the carry into bit 31 and the carry out of bit 31.

Signed overflow happens when the carry into bit 31 and the carry out of bit 31 are different.

Shifts (like MIPS)

Type the value, press the shift, type the shift amount, then press =. The shift amount is always typed in decimal (0 to 31), as in MIPS: sll $t0, $t1, 4.

sllShift left logical. 0s fill from the right.
srlShift right logical. 0s fill from the left.
sraShift right arithmetic. Copies of bit 31 (the sign bit) fill from the left.

Multiply and divide with shifts

Shifting by n bits multiplies or divides by 2, 4, 8, 16, ... (2 to the power n). Example: n = 3 means × 8 or ÷ 8. MIPS has no "sla": sll works for both signed and unsigned values.

sll nMultiply, signed or unsigned. 5 sll 3 = 40 (5 × 8). −5 sll 2 = −20 (−5 × 4). Bits shifted out of bit 31 are lost.
srl nDivide an unsigned value. 0xF0000000 srl 4 = 0x0F000000 (4026531840 ÷ 16 = 251658240).
sra nDivide a signed value. −64 sra 3 = −8 (−64 ÷ 8).

Warning 1: do not use srl on a negative value. −64 srl 3 = 536870904, not −8.

Warning 2: if a negative value does not divide exactly, sra rounds down, not toward zero. −7 sra 1 = −4, but MIPS div gives −7 ÷ 2 = −3. Word addresses and word offsets divide exactly by 4, so this does not affect them.

Tip: to type −5, tap the DEC signed row, type 5, then press NEG.

One-value operations (act right away)

NOTFlip all 32 bits
NEGTwo's complement negate (0 − value)
SE16Sign-extend the low 16 bits to 32 bits (like the immediate of addi, lw, sw, and like lh)
ZE16Zero-extend the low 16 bits to 32 bits (like the immediate of andi, ori, xori, and like lhu)
SE8Sign-extend the low 8 bits to 32 bits (like lb)
ZE8Zero-extend the low 8 bits to 32 bits (like lbu)

Two's complement by hand: NEG is the same as NOT and then + 1. Try it: type 5, press NOT, then press + 1 =. The result is −5, the same as 5 NEG.

Other keys

ACClear everything
⌫Delete the last typed digit
UndoGo back one step. Press it again to go back more steps (up to 50). It can also undo AC.
RedoGo forward again after Undo, one step at a time. Use Undo and Redo to step back and forth through your work. A new change after Undo clears the Redo steps.
BitsTap any bit in the BIN area to flip it between 0 and 1
